\## Hi, I’m Vikum.



I’m a mathematics researcher and educator at Clemson University, where I’m continuing doctoral study in Mathematical Sciences.



My interests include combinatorics, discrete dynamical systems, graph theory, cryptography, and optimization. I use Python to explore mathematical examples, visualize patterns, and test conjectures.

My mathematical training spans probability, stochastic processes, linear algebra, optimization, and discrete mathematics. I’m also interested in applying mathematical and computational methods to quantitative research in financial markets.



\## Research and Projects



\### Bulgarian Solitaire with Distinguishable Cells



Working with Dr. Svetlana Poznanović, I study Generalizations of Bulgarian solitaire with distinguishable cells.



A central question is how a configuration can return to its original shape before its individual cells return to their original positions. Our work connects cell movement on Ferrers diagrams with permutation cycles and periodic orbit lengths.



I use Python for computational experiments and visualization. This research has led to our coauthored preprint, \*Bulgarian Solitaire with Distinguishable Cells\*.



\### Collatz Conjecture–Driven Cryptography



I have investigated cryptographic constructions inspired by Collatz sequences, including implementing proposed encryption and decryption algorithms in Python.



This work led to the coauthored paper \*Collatz Conjecture–Driven Cryptographic Framework\*, published in the \*\*Journal of Discrete Mathematical Sciences \& Cryptography in 2026\*\*.



\### Graph Theory and Symmetric Encryption



I have also studied graph-labeling constructions involving snake graphs and supermagic coverings for symmetric encryption.



This work led to the coauthored paper \*Symmetric Encryption Using Snake Graphs and Supermagic Covering\*, published in the \*\*Journal of the National Science Foundation of Sri Lanka in 2024\*\*.
